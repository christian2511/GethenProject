Can Terrans Constructively Engage With Gethen?

In The Left Hand of Darkness (1969), by Ursula K. Le Guin, a female-bodied (ambiguous gender) society on a far-off planet is brought into the fold of the Ekumen, an interstellar federation of worlds, on a mission to bring the inhabitants of the icy Gethen to a united front in the cosmic galaxy. While the novel presents a utopian resolution arising from the empathy and self-sacrifice of an individual Envoy, the real-world implementation of such a diplomatic mission, based on a pragmatic audit of the political and social systems of present-day Earth, would, I estimate, result in something significantly less heartfelt. Gethen's challenges would lead to military campaigns and forcible assimilation, ideological repudiation rather than diplomatic union, if the Terran powers had their way.

Any diplomatic pursuit of peaceful first contact presupposes the idea that Earth has a single political or moral voice. Today's human nations have yet to reach consensus on any singular global threat, ranging from climate change to ecological destruction to nuclear proliferation. In the realm of foreign policy, international institutions such as the UN are designed to serve as “institutions that would speak on behalf of humanity” (Carter) in potential cases of first contact; however, these organizations are hamstrung in their ability to speak authoritatively on global issues by the constraints of state sovereignty. As legal scholar Michael J. Glennon shows in his critique of international law, international organizations “are now of limited usefulness, if not harmful, and are, in fact, nothing more than 'today's universe of superpower wrangling, vetoes, and superpower self-interest of the underdog'” (Glennon 16). As a result, even in the face of alien discovery, Gethen would not be perceived as the agent of planetary peace but rather as a potential goose to be shot in the solar system's zero-sum game of extraterrestrial technological mastery.

In addition to existing in a form of structural political fragmentation, Terran hostility would originate from the psychological and cultural intolerance of Gethenian biology. Gethenians are ambisexual sequential hermaphrodites, who do not experience nor reproduce in one specific binary gender identity and are constantly or alternately in fertile kemmer (Le Guin 91). Human structural dualism, gender essentialism, and the notion of fixed binary genders would be inescapable in the human paradigm. Even Genly Ai, through all his training and political discipline, is unable to see the Gethenians without placing on them his own cultural assumptions, as the scholar Elizabeth Cummins points out: “The Gethenians 'expose him... Completely unprotected to the influence of the alien' and he must acknowledge 'that he was a man, and yet a woman'” (Cummins 73). In his report to his home government, Ai insists that Gethenian behaviors like lying and passivity are ascribed to the lack of human biological gender. In the process he pathologizes the Gethenian notion of gender expression, simply on its lack of being human. Ai's myopic gaze into the subversion of old gender signifiers makes visible to us “the inarticability of the human paradigm.., based as it is on its own unconscious fears of otherness” (Cummins 73). By extension, if this diplomat from an advanced civilisation prevaricates at the sight of ambisexual humans, the Terran media and politicians, already plagued with fear over “unnatural” sexual deviances in their own society, would instinctively associate Gethen's ambisexuality with an existential social challenge.

In addition, there is an almost complete structural incongruity between Gethenian social structure and human military theory. Absence of war in Gethen is a biologico-political marker of its political system; to a Gethenian, even political assassination and intra-tribal, feuding prahs are more consensual than the competition, invasion and conquest that mark human political history (Le Guin 96). The theorists of the status quo system to sustain international insecurity, structural realists, hold that states in an anarchic system will inevitably recognize a world without military power as a destabilizing phenomenon, and that the first actors of an international order of this sort would seek to secure an advantage on the basis of their military weakness (Mearsheimer 31). In this context, an invasion force from Earth's military powers would misinterpret the political serenity of Gethen, as well as its complex, multilevel preeminence hierarchy of shifgrethor, as an indicator that the alien people are an army's doormat. Rather than respecting the Gethenian society, Earth's military empire would attempt to force Gethen into submission through occupying strategic locations and controlling indigenous peoples.

The desire for total control would be compounded by their imperialist notion of Gethen's environment and resources. As in The Left Hand of Darkness, the extreme winter of Gethen is a life of hardship, which awakens “in a community of people whose bodies show signs that they survive, all their energy is directed toward that one purpose, in contrast to the Terrestrial industrial nations, which, unlike the Gethenians, must be driven by economics toward an unlimited expansion of their resources and their borders.” Gethen, however, provides no economic benefit to the Pan-Arctic powers; with an entire planet unprotected, this “planned effort at colony building” would be conducted by their space organizations, both corporate and public, who would see the planet as “an unexploited resource, to be secured by moving in the massive steel factories, machinery, and prestige of the civilized nations, carrying the flag, bringing the light, to a land that is unloved.” By the same logic, as they had in the history of Earth, the Pan-Arctic countries would rationalize the locals as the biologically or socially “inferior” inhabitants of a “less-evolved” planet, thereby justifying total subjugation and resource extraction.

In the end, the structural arrangements of human civilization—its binary thinking, its aggressiveness and tendency towards international competition and war, its state-sanctioned violence against those seen as different—would make meaningful contact with a Gethenian civilization nearly impossible. Le Guin's novel indicates that first contact is a process which requires the individual to shed institutional biases and transform him/herself into a vulnerable being, to willingly embrace otherness and difference, even if it is that of an alien race. Only then is true contact possible. Until Earthlings came to recognize and have dominion over their own conflicts and to abolish militarism, international pursuit of power, and essentialism regarding biology, contact with “the other” would lead to war, not peace.

Status of Military Forays Between Karhide and Orgoreyn

From
Mobile Investigator (Envoy) to the Hearth of the Ekumen
Subject
Status Report: Border Dynamics and Military Posture between Karhide and Orgoreyn
Planet
Gethen (Winter)

Overview

In line with our principle of observation and non-intervention, this office has been vigilant for any changes in the political situation and the military buildup of the two major sovereign countries, Karhide and Orgoreyn.

As familiar to the Ekumen, Gethenian history is based on shifting loyalties, waxing and waning seasonal moods, and the absence of standing professional armies in the old Hainish or Terran fashion. Yet new modes of institutional mobilization have arisen due to the ongoing geopolitical tensions along the northern and eastern frontiers.

Current Posture: Karhide

Karhide is not centrally governed, but extends as a patchwork of clan lordships, through the Karhidish network of kin and town responsibilities and the powers of the King and Prime Minister.

  • Militia and Local Levies: Karhide does not have a standing army. Defense and local excursions are made up of regional levies called up by local landholders (domain-captains) or assembled by fiat.
  • Tactical Reality: Mobilization is hampered by seasonal passes, frigid temperatures, and the logistical challenge of transporting supplies over rugged mountains.
  • Recent Activity: Cross-border activity has consisted of border skirmishing, shows of force around contested farming or mining areas, and security sweeps through the Orgoreyn-Karhide border region. The mindset continues to be defensive and territorial rather than expansionist.

Current Posture: Orgoreyn

Orgoreyn operates as a bureaucratic, collectivist apparatus running on the Thirty-Three Land-Commands and steered by a directorate of administrators, headed by the Comman.

  • Security Structure: Orgoreyn employs a more structured internal security force and disciplined labor militias. Like its neighbor, it does not have a standing professional army, but maintains a superior ability to regiment manpower for state purposes.
  • The Voluntary Camps: The state's re-education and work camps maintain the façade of community welfare and productivity while remaining “voluntary” in name only. They combine ideological control with a supply of regulated, ready-to-mobilize labor for boundary zone infrastructure projects.
  • Tactical Trends: Orgoreyn prefers diplomatic-bureaucratic expansion that transitions swiftly into tactical containment. In place of open field engagements, they employ administrative encroachment, trade pressure, and state surveillance forces to claim and hold the contested frontier.

Analysis from the Ekumen

The military “forays” between Karhide and Orgoreyn do not constitute total, annihilatory wars. Instead, they serve as structural friction where two fundamentally opposed social systems collide at their borders.

  1. The Nature of Conflict: Centuries of Gethenian civilization have little precedent for permanent institutionalized hostility. The war on Winter is cold, short, and deeply destabilizing to the delicate, tenuous trading and harvesting systems of the planet.
  2. Resource Constraints: Neither society possesses the war-economy resources to sustain extended campaigns, despite Karhide's royal-feudal individualism and Orgoreyn's bureaucratic collectivism standing on opposite sides of a yawning ideological chasm. However, the introduction of modern political paranoia at the state level has rendered these localized clashes significantly deadlier.

We continue to maintain observation posts near the borders, committed to peaceful mediation through cultural understanding while standing ready for any abrupt escalation in scale.
